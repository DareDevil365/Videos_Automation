# Write a connected short story

The owner rated the earlier Vision/Spider-Man writing **2/10**. Those versions remain technical history. Their short plots and passing render checks are not an approved writing reference. The complaint was about disconnected summarization, insufficient story and missing transitions, especially between the Ship of Theseus analogy and the memory unlock.

## Work before the script

Read the chosen source packet from the [source library](../../../research/content-lore/SOURCE_LIBRARY.md). Identify what the protagonist wants, what prevents it, what choice changes the situation and what that choice causes. Keep the exact issue, run or episode separate from adaptations. A publisher teaser rarely supplies this entire chain.

Write a small ledger for each beat with four fields: state before it, new fact or decision, state after it, and why the next beat follows. Link factual details to claim IDs. If the next step requires an unstated motive or rule, read that specific scene and update the packet before writing. Preserve the decisive connection instead of replacing it with “then.”

## Turn the ledger into narration

- Open on the actual problem while identifying the source near the start. Show the matching cover or poster during narration. Avoid a silent logo pre-roll. Name the comic and issue for comics, and the show and episode for television.
- Keep the motive alive through the action. Peter's transfusion makes the medical emergency personal; the stolen serum leads him to the trap; remembering Ben changes his decision to keep pushing.
- Explain an analogy's application. For Vision, present the replaced ship, the restored old planks, and both identity claims. Explicitly map that puzzle back to the two bodies and their memory data before the unlock.
- Use enough specific events to make the result earned. Do not merely list a premise, an impressive feat and a conclusion. Avoid repeating the takeaway after the result.
- Give a closing consequence when it adds information. White Vision's survival does not erase Wanda's goodbye to the partner inside her world. That distinction is part of this revised ending, not a prediction about later shows.
- Read the complete text as continuous speech. Each sentence must pick up a problem, decision or consequence the viewer already understands. A short sentence can land a turn; eight chopped highlights do not create continuity.

## Duration and pace

For the owner's current standalone revisions, request `source_duration: [42, 50]` and aim close to 50 seconds. Draft a fuller story, normally around 150–190 words, then measure the actual selected voices. Voice and sentence structure change the result; word count is not a stopwatch.

`source_delivery.speed` supports bounded 0.9–1.25× delivery. Use a faster value only while names and the causal explanation remain intelligible. This revision uses 1.24× for the slower Vision pairing and 1.15× for Spider-Man. Keep linked comic titles and character names together. Short pauses can give an earned thought time to land.

The duration window is enforced on measured speech before the visual render. Requests with this window do not use the legacy near-minimum slowdown correction. If the story is too short, revise its content. If it runs long, remove repetition or excess wording before removing the bridge that explains the ending. Never truncate speech or add dead time to satisfy the target.

## Review before rendering

Cold-read without the title, poster or music. Explain the story's causal chain to a newcomer. Identify the first omitted explanation, ambiguous pronoun, unsupported detail or redundant sentence. Verify that the final action resolves the opening problem. Measure speech after the words are stable, then inspect actual captions and contact/action frames.

Save the review as editorial reasoning, not as proof that the script is engaging. Technical QA, schema compliance and source checks each cover a different problem. Only the owner and later audience response can establish whether the writing improved enough.
