# Research and reach for comic-lore Shorts

## Choose an angle before drafting

1. Read `production-history`. Check the last few episodes and uploaded titles. Identify which topic, question, character and setting would feel new to that audience.
2. Pitch a few one-sentence angles. Prefer a recognizable character and a specific decision, object, rule or reversal. “Iron Man's legacy” names a subject. “Why did Pepper keep the reactor Tony wanted to throw away?” identifies a scene and promises an answer.
3. Search for the exact question and a few natural variations. Record public comparison URLs, publication dates, displayed counts and the date observed. Compare videos of similar length and age where possible. Write down what the titles promise and whether the payoff appears crowded or underserved. Treat public views as a rough demand signal. They do not reveal impressions, retention, upload promotion, or why a video performed.
4. Open publisher recaps, official on-screen biographies, issue pages or the original work if available. Label the continuity and date. Check the turning point against a second source. Use the brief to separate facts, interpretation, and illustrative staging. Keep one small claim per ID and attach it to the exact script beat.
5. Pick the angle with the best combination of audience recognition, a fresh answer, and a visually demonstrable payoff. Record that decision in the episode notes.

## Design shots people can follow quickly

- Make the first frame and first sentence tell the same story. A viewer should understand the conflict or odd detail on mute. Start with the important object or action already in frame.
- Turn the explanation into a visible chain. Show who or what starts the action, the point of contact or change, and the result. Use a wide or environmental frame for setup, a clean detail for the clue, and a contrasting shot for the payoff. Keep the main event easy to spot at phone size.
- Use geometric characters as the style anchor. Add clean source cutouts for objects, places, textures or details that gain from being recognizable. Treat the photo and graphic parts as one composition and match their scale and light. Use a photo character when it has a specific editorial purpose.
- Review with sound and on mute. Check the first second, name pronunciation, every new scene, the decisive action and the ending. A caption, effect or lower reaction panel should leave the focal point clear. If a shot repeats, give it a story reason, such as the same object returning with a new meaning.
- Write a title with the actual character, topic or question. Choose one supported promise. The description should give viewers the film or comic name and what the video covers, using phrases people would naturally search. Include real credits and sources through the package.

## After publication

Use Studio to record **Stayed to watch**, engaged views, average view duration, average percentage viewed and the first meaningful retention drop. Compare with similar videos on this channel, at a similar age. Record the hypothesis, the video ID and the next adjustment. Change one major variable when comparing follow-ups if possible. A public view count is useful for discovery research. The channel's own viewing data is what tests whether the opening and payoff worked.

Sources for the metrics and discovery process are [YouTube Shorts search and discovery](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/11914225?co=YOUTUBE._YTVideoType%3Dshorts&hl=en), [Shorts analytics](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12942217?co=YOUTUBE._YTVideoType%3Dshorts&hl=en), and [audience retention](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415?hl=en).


Latest owner priority: audience interest and growth come before convenient sources/assets or a particular movie/comic format. Follow the expanded [topic decision process](TOPIC_SELECTION.md), compare at least five premises, and preserve rejection reasons. A movie recap under a minute is an option when its conflict and payoff justify it. Record choices with evidence; public view counts and model scores cannot establish future retention.
