# Comic-lore shot decisions and assistant task list

The assistant chooses the story, checks the evidence, writes the beats and directs what the viewer sees. The program validates the plan, resolves registered assets, measures narration, draws and animates supported scene recipes, renders, checks the export and packages it. Antigravity can author the inputs using its active model. The assistant-authored route uses those supplied inputs without a separate story-writing call.

Use the creative choices below as defaults. Adapt pacing, framing and density to the story and the owner's latest direction. Source support, asset eligibility and truthful publication state are separate requirements.

## Work from the last known state

1. **Resume or choose.** Run `production-history --limit 5` through the project CLI. Read `latest_made`, `recent_unfinished`, `uploaded_through_app`, `upload_needs_reconciliation` and `next_to_make`. For a revision, verify the exact build with `batch-status --plan ...`, open the latest MP4 and identify the affected beat IDs. Continue the same initialized plan for an interrupted run. Use a new build ID for changed inputs and use `--revise-batch` for a revision of the same preview series. For a fresh topic, treat the queue as an idea source alongside the owner's priorities. [Assistant handoff](../../ASSISTANT_PRODUCTION.md)
2. **Research an angle and build a claim chain.** Review recent topics and their openings. Choose a specific question, decision, reversal or object with an answer the viewer can see. Identify the continuity, title and year. Use the issue, film or publisher material where accessible and corroborate the decisive claim. Save a short brief, one claim per ID and source limitations. Separate verified events, interpretations and illustrative blocking. Comparable public videos can inform topic choice. Record them as demand clues and use owned analytics to assess the result later. [Research and reach](RESEARCH_AND_REACH.md)
3. **Write the script.** Start with a concrete visual question. Give just enough context for the character's action to make sense. Build from setup to action to consequence. Give the payoff the time it needs and carry a real unanswered question across parts only when the series calls for it. Link each factual beat to its supporting claim IDs. Use the host for opening and closing where the format calls for it. Record the exact source review and hashes after the script is frozen. [Comic-lore creation workflow](HOW_TO_CREATE.md)
4. **Direct each beat.** Write one sentence about what the viewer must see change. Choose the people, their relationship in the frame, the prop's state, camera, movement and consequence. Decide where hands, eyelines and effects connect. Use the matrix below. A spoken character name can justify an introduction, a reaction or neither, depending on whether the viewer needs that person's identity to understand the action.
5. **Assemble and budget.** Check the existing scene recipes and prepared asset inventory. First try a different shot with reused characters, props and sets. Use registered object cutouts when shape, texture or readable details matter. For a missing photo, use eligible geometry or shorten the idea to a supported shot. Build one new reusable scene when the viewer would otherwise miss the hook or causal turn. Test its early, contact and late frames before rendering the full edit. [Starter asset folder](../../../Resources/Templates/ComicLore/README.md), [asset inventory and reuse plan](REUSABLE_ASSET_PLAN.md)
6. **Check evidence and framing together.** Show supported events and label any reconstructed blocking when it could be mistaken for a literal reenactment. In the reactor story, Pepper leaves the gift and Tony discovers it. That preserves her action without depicting a direct face-to-face handoff. Let the receiving and departing hands, the empty socket or the recipient's reaction establish causality. Keep names, signatures and quoted inscriptions accurate to the source.
7. **Plan, render and inspect.** Run the assistant-authored preview workflow with the frozen source files. Review the actual MP4 at phone size and on mute. The opening should communicate the question, motion should make contact, and a visible consequence should follow. With sound, confirm clear names, natural pauses, readable captions and quiet cues. Review the saved technical report for duration, portrait dimensions, audio and decode. A technically passing render still needs the creative check. Fix the failed stage or affected beats rather than rewriting an already supported story. [Render commands](../../ASSISTANT_PRODUCTION.md)
8. **Package and log.** Write a title that promises the video's actual payoff and a concise description that identifies the continuity. For the optional packaging contract, provide the title options, chosen title and exact supporting script quotes. Check the packaged sources, credits, captions and video path. Confirm `latest_made` shows the finished local export. Give the owner its path and note relevant creative or source limitations. [Title and description playbook](../../TITLE_DESCRIPTION_PLAYBOOK.md)
9. **Hand off publication when requested.** A preview is a local result. Follow the publishing workflow for a separate eligible production with the agreed visibility, cleared assets and exact final package. If the channel connection or a transfer is unresolved, keep the saved job and reconcile it. Record an uploaded video by its returned YouTube ID and confirm its actual status. [Publishing and recovery](../../AUTOMATIC_PRODUCTION.md)

## Decide what belongs on screen

| Signal in the beat | Shot and asset choice | Timing and continuity | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The hook makes a viewer ask about an object | Start with a close-up or push on the object, with enough background to place it. The reactor episode starts on the dim display case. | The question reads in the first frame. Hold the recognizable shape while the host delivers the question. Save the inscription for the end. | A logo intro, an empty wide shot or an immediate spoiler of the payoff. |
| Location or time changed and matters | Use a brief wide or pan to establish it. Show the cave walls and battery before the reactor build. Carry an object or direction into the next view. | Establish geography before the action starts. If the location is already obvious, move directly to the action. | A new establishing shot for each line, or a location change that looks like the object teleported. |
| A character's identity changes the stakes | Put the geometric figure in frame using their silhouette, posture, hair, clothing or signature piece. Tony's workwear, swept hair and goatee contrast with Stane's broad suit, bald head and gray beard. Pepper has her own hair and jacket. | Let the viewer read those cues near the name or first action. A clean caption or portrait marker can help when the silhouettes are still ambiguous. | Three identical bodies distinguished only by color. A large face badge competing with the action. |
| An exchange is central and both people must be seen | Use a two-shot if the source supports shared space. If the exchange is indirect, match-cut around the prop. Pepper puts the case on the bench, then Tony appears and reaches toward the same case. | Show giver, prop contact with the surface, then recipient. Preserve the prop's placement and direction across the cut. | A floating box, hands that never reach it, or staging a direct handoff from evidence of a gift alone. |
| The action is a theft, removal or deliberate choice | Use a medium two-shot so the viewer sees who acts and who is affected. Stane reaches for Tony's reactor, takes it, and moves it away. Tony recoils. | Show the initial state, approach, hand-to-object contact, separation and aftermath. The glow follows the reactor. Tony's empty socket stays visible. | A detached reactor moving before the hand arrives, an effect that hides the grip, or a cut before the result reads. |
| The narration explains a mechanism | Use a tight insert or clean diagram. Show the cave battery cable feeding the chest magnet, then the reactor assembly. Show the output as well as the input. | Animate in the order the explanation unfolds. Use the next cut to show what the mechanism allows Tony to do. | A generic glowing ring for every line or a diagram with more elements than the narration explains. |
| A small device matters while the character is still in danger | Keep the character in the wide scene and connect a circular detail to the relevant object or location. The Doom episode uses this for Strange's escape device. | Reveal the detail as the narration identifies it. Then show what it changes for the character. | An inset that covers the action or a precise object design presented as a verified comic prop. |
| The object returns with new meaning | Match its signature shape or position, then show its new use. The old reactor leaves the case and the story returns to Tony's chest, this time with the pulse restored. | Give the restored state a brief hold so the viewer can connect it to the earlier setup. | Changing the original prop's identifying details, or confusing the old reactor with the upgrade Stane takes. |
| A material detail proves the payoff | Use an original geometric actor for story action and a clean registered object cutout for a specific insert. The replica photo lets the viewer read the reactor's inscription. | Reveal it when the host reaches the closing line. Keep enough clear screen space and time to read it. Preserve the asset's attribution and eligibility. | Using a photo solely because one exists, or suggesting a replica is the original screen-used prop. |
| A face is hard to recognize on a quick cut | Start with silhouette and clothing. Use a small circular identity marker if a registered face image adds clarity. Where available, use a saved crop and geometry fallback. | Introduce once on a readable shot. Bring the marker back only when the viewer needs reorientation. Check rights before using it in a publication candidate. | Face matching per frame, unregistered images or identifiers blocking hands and captions. |
| The exact movement is unclear or beyond the editor | Show the supported before and after with a clear visual connection, or choose a close-up or wide view that makes the known event understandable. For an essential action, budget a small authored scene and document its illustrative scope. | Time the visual change around the supported verb. Keep uncertain details out of the literal staging. | An invented event, exact motion or dialogue used to fill a missing visual. |
| A reaction, twist or ending has landed | Cut to the affected person, changed object or result only if it adds information. The lower workshop strip can support atmosphere quietly. | Allow the audience to read the consequence before leaving. Simplify the strip when the upper action needs attention. | A reaction after every sentence or motion that distracts from the ending. |

## Translate the shot into the current editor

| Current control | Use it for |
| --- | --- |
| `lore_scenes` | Map an existing recipe to each beat ID. The Iron Man example uses `reactor_gift` for `u5`, `reactor_theft` for `u6` and `reactor_return` for `u7`. Custom blocking inside those scenes is drawn by the recipe. |
| `lore_cameras` | Select `wide`, `push`, `pan_left`, `pan_right` or `detail`. Use a camera change to show a new relationship or detail. The authored gift scene uses a wide enough frame for the bench and character. |
| `source_panels` | Put an eligible registered cutout in a pixel `box`. Use `motion` when a supported cutout transform tells the story. Use `repeat_reason` when a return to the same image is essential to the payoff. |
| `lore_actions` | Select a supported local effect and specify `origin` and, where useful, `target`. Use `actor: effect_only` when the underlying recipe already draws the actor. Check that the effect attaches to the hand or object in the export. |
| `source_delivery`, `source_cues`, `source_music` | Shape the narration and mix. Use `linked_phrases` for names, `pause_after` and `pauses_after` for breathing room, and cue effects sparingly. The current source cues land near the start of the beat. A precise hand contact uses the visual recipe and actual timed narration. |
| `reaction_preset` | Choose an installed lower set when it supports the scene. A quiet setting can be useful throughout a chapter. It should leave the primary change and captions easy to see. |

For a shot plan, record the beat ID, viewer takeaway, claim IDs, scene and camera, cast and identifying cues, prop and initial state, contact or trigger, consequence, and any provenance or fallback. Use a normalized moment inside the beat for your own direction and then verify it against the measured narration. These planning notes may include choices that have no direct JSON control. The [editor controls](VISUAL_STYLE.md) and [Iron Man recipe](../../../examples/lore-series/iron-man-reactor/series.json) show what can be passed to the current plan.

The [animation reference notes](ANIMATION_REFERENCE_NOTES.md) record three sampled examples supplied by the owner and how to apply their staging to this format.

The [Hellboy shot list](../../../examples/lore-series/hellboy-horns/README.md) demonstrates the reverse hybrid arrangement. Keep all living actors geometric, including NPCs, while registered photographs supply scenery and the book prop. Its horn action shows reach, contact, separation and the remaining stubs. Different framing and a changed state make the return to lava a purposeful callback. The scene-specific `hellboy_media` control supplies the three photographic layers; it does not automatically choose settings or draw a new cast. The assistant must still specify and inspect those choices.


For the medium of each layer, use the [script-focus visual decision rules](VISUAL_DECISIONS.md). Record purpose before choosing artwork. A hot-mask fitting can use geometric Doom; a Doom introduction can use a cutout. Complexity and lookalike costumes affect recognition, while the exact contact or material change affects animation. The same guide explains voice rotation and its fixed per-series assignment.


For every new production, complete [topic selection](TOPIC_SELECTION.md) and [art direction](ART_DIRECTION.md). The assistant must record a reason for story, cast/prop inclusion, medium, location, palette, font, motion, camera, music and ending. Scene color follows the action/context: rising warm embers for a forge, multicolor energy for a Hex context, rain/earth for a stormy graveyard, or quiet daylight for aftermath. Preserve recognizable costumes and source art; do not apply one tint to everything. Keep the bottom audience explicit and verify it in the exported frames.
