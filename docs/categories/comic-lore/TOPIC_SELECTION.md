# Choose a story someone wants to watch

The channel's purpose is audience growth through videos people choose and continue watching. A valid source, famous character, working renderer or completed video is insufficient. The owner rejected the Vision identity discussion and the earlier machinery rescue as topic choices. Preserve that feedback when choosing the next story. Do not keep proposing a topic merely because the assets already exist.

## Research before committing to a script

1. Read the latest owner feedback, production history and recent topics. Search the local source library for possible stories; it is an inventory, not the release schedule.
2. Develop at least five distinct premises. Say what happens, not just a character name or a theme. Include the character's immediate danger/desire, what changes, and the consequential ending. A full-film recap under a minute is an allowed format, not a priority above an interesting story.
3. Ask of each premise: would a newcomer understand the situation in one sentence? Is there a concrete reason to want the next event? Does the story become more interesting after its opening? Does a choice, discovery, betrayal or reversal change the situation? Is the ending worth the setup? How much explanation must precede anything happening? Reject a thin or purely abstract premise before spending time rendering it.
4. Search exact and alternate angles on YouTube and in comic discussion. Open representative results when accessible. Record source URLs, observation date, actual accessible title/date/count, and what is unknown. Existing coverage is evidence of competition, not proof of demand or an underserved opportunity. Never invent a count, use an unrelated popular clip as proof, or assume a view cause from correlation. Distinguish film, games, canon, adaptations and fan stories.
5. Read publisher material, the actual issue where accessible, creator discussion or a licensed preview. Extract motive → obstacle → decision → action → consequence, including the actual ending. A teaser cannot establish a twist. Cache reviewed facts with source IDs; record inaccessible pages and missing facts. Do not copy another creator's script or treat a recap as an official panel.
6. Compare the surviving premises in plain language. For every choice record evidence coverage, opening conflict, escalation, payoff, familiarity, recent repetition, competition, visual opportunities and obstacles. Avoid arbitrary numerical scores that pretend to measure future views. Select for audience interest first, then plan how to execute it. If a compelling story needs a small new scene, add it; do not automatically replace it with a boring supported scene.
7. Draft two or three genuinely different openings for the selected story. State the exact question each leaves open and what pays it off. Cold-read the complete story without art or soundtrack. Remove the first confusing or redundant passage and verify that necessary causal bridges survive. Keep the source identification near the opening while the conflict is already underway.
8. Save `SELECTION_REVIEW.md` with accepted/rejected alternatives, research links and limitations. The assistant makes and records this editorial decision; the local renderer does not discover the best story automatically. A model's favorable verdict is not an audience verdict.

## What counts as a reason to continue

| Story situation | Question the next beat must advance | Failure to reject |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A hero is trapped while someone impersonates them | How can they escape, and what does the impostor want? | Repeating that the trap is dangerous without a new event |
| A familiar hero makes a disturbing choice | What pressure caused it, and what does it cost? | A sensational headline whose cause is never explained |
| A supposedly weaker person defeats a stronger one | What rule or decision changes the contest? | An unsupported power-ranking list |
| A gift or small object becomes decisive | Why did that object survive, and what can it now do? | An ordinary prop called important only in the final sentence |
| An entire film is recapped | What connects its main problem, decisions, reversal and ending? | A rapid list of names and disconnected scenes |

## Learn after publication

Use the connected channel's actual impressions/Shorts feed behavior, stayed-to-watch, retention, engaged views, average duration and subscription response. Store the video ID, observation window, first drop and next hypothesis. Until that exists, describe story choices as editorial judgments and public references as limited clues. Growth is the objective; a craft checklist cannot certify reach. Avoid repeating rejected premises in another costume.
