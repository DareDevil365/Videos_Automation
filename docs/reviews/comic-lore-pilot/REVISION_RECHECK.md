# Revised Secret Wars pilot: script and visual recheck

Reviewed 6 November 2026. **Current scripts are clear for a prototype render within the available recap evidence. Visual improvements are still recommended.** This is not publication approval, an audience-performance score or a claim that the final episodes have been watched. [The structured revision record](revision-critique.json) preserves current script hashes and inspected sample hashes; the [initial critique](SCRIPT_CRITIQUE.md) remains a separate record of the older drafts.

## Script changes checked

Part 1 now ends with new information, rather than paraphrasing the opening's control/worship claim. It removes the unexplained Future Foundation name and reaches Battleworld directly. Reed's identity is still assumed, but there are fewer simultaneous new names.

An additional factual ambiguity appeared in the first revision: after introducing Reed's life-raft survivors, the script called “the survivors” subjects who forgot their old reality. That wording could incorrectly attach the memory loss to the outsiders who remembered the former world. The current u6 names Battleworld's people; u7 explicitly distinguishes the remembering raft survivors. Claim c13 now records the exception. This removes that blocker. [Marvel's Miles biography](https://www.marvel.com/characters/spider-man-miles-morales/in-comics) supports the remembering-survivors distinction; the direct canonical page remains inaccessible, with the official indexed text consulted.

Part 2 now identifies Miles as Spider-Man and gets to the hidden source without the redundant raft recap or Owen Reece/Doomstadt naming pileup. The u6/u7 `visual_query` labels now match their `onscreen` labels. The prior mother-loss is planted before the last part. Claim c12 fairly supports that prior loss because Marvel describes her as previously deceased before restoration. This is a reasonable narrative paraphrase of [the official biography](https://www.marvel.com/characters/spider-man-miles-morales/in-comics), not a newly invented tragedy.

One delivery caution remains: u6 follows the food gift with Miles's loss and the recipient's powers. Do not make the voice or images imply Miles calculated a resurrection reward. Keep it an ordinary kindness whose consequence he did not anticipate.

Part 3 removes the Franklin/Foundation name stack and replaces the generic final moral with a concrete burger/family callback. Its power-transfer chain and Miles's reward are now distinguishable. The opening states both final outcomes, so the episode's interest depends on **how they happen**, not pretending viewers still do not know whether they happen. The [official Secret Wars guide](https://www.marvel.com/articles/comics/secret-wars-official-marvel-guide) supports the admission and power transfer; the mother reward is a separate consequence. No remaining definite canon contradiction was established from the accessible official recaps. Full comic issues were not read.

## Actual visual samples inspected

Inspected the ten-scene [gallery](../../../Resources/Drafts/comic-lore-renderer/contact-sheet.png), full-resolution [hidden-power image](../../../Resources/Drafts/comic-lore-renderer/hidden_power.png), and frames at **0.0, 0.5, 1.0 and 1.5 seconds** from the two-second [silent renderer sample](../../../Resources/Drafts/comic-lore-renderer/hidden-power-preview.mp4). The watch-video extraction confirmed a 720×1280 video with no audio. These are genuine sample timestamps, not estimated episode attention-drop times.

### High: the discovery is already complete at the start

Across the sampled two seconds, the main composition barely changes. A small pulse, star particles and gentle movement provide atmosphere, but the viewer does not discover anything. The full diagram is already shown. Holding that completed image through a longer narration beat risks feeling static.

Stage the information: show the throne, reveal the space below it, identify the hidden person, then visibly carry the power upward. This makes the actual event legible rather than asking narration to animate a finished chart.

### High: person identity and power direction are ambiguous

The lower figure is a small generic icon. At first glance it resembles a signage person rather than an identifiable character. The vertical cyan connection does not have an unmistakable direction; without the narration I could read it as the ruler powering the captive below instead of drawing power from him.

Use necessary identity labels—Doom and Molecule Man—and an upward arrow or travelling pulse. Increase the meaningful figure scale and preserve consistent role shapes/colors across scenes. Labels should explain the actual relationship, not become decorative channel text.

### Medium: presentation still feels more like an explainer than comic drama

The ten scenes do have different compositions: worlds, a throne, food and power transfer. However, the common starfield, flat small figures and similar panel treatment dominate the gallery. There is little character intention visible. The food gesture and family return are especially dependent on narration to establish who is involved and why it matters.

Vary focal scale, angle and staged action; use a close view for the gesture and a clearer relational composition for the return. Code-drawn symbolic panels are an honest production choice, but should be described as such. They do not become publisher art or literal animated character acting merely because the border resembles a comic panel.

The full sample also reserves a substantial lower area. That may be needed for final captions; it is not yet a proven layout defect. Check the composed episode rather than assuming that the uncaptioned sample's empty area is its final presentation.

## Still pending

The final narrated pilot must be reviewed for actual speech clarity, caption/diagram competition, reading time, music balance and attention over the whole episode. A silent two-second template cannot establish those qualities. This recheck supports moving to a prototype, with the visual risks above recorded, rather than calling the series creatively finished.
