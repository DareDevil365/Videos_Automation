# Secret Wars pilot: independent audience critique

Reviewed 5 November 2026. **Recommendation: rewrite before rendering.** This is a script-only review of the three initial drafts. No finished video, performance, sound mix or real audience behavior was observed. The two simulated reader roles below are critique lenses, not two human panelists or measured retention data. Exact reviewed file hashes and ten unit-level issues are in [the structured report](script-critique.json).

## First-time viewer

The series has a useful central contradiction: the villain rescues people and makes their survival serve him. The current drafts explain that contradiction repeatedly instead of turning it into enough discoveries and consequences. The main attention problem is exposition density, not a lack of dramatic adjectives.

### Part 1: a strong opening without a new enough ending

**Likely first attention loss: u3.** “Reed Richards and the Future Foundation built a life raft for the end.” A newcomer has just heard about universes colliding. They now have a person, an organization and an unexplained survival object to hold in memory before seeing what the new world is. The organization name does no work in this episode. Remove it, introduce Reed by his relevant relationship if he is needed, or save the survival detail for a moment when it changes the conflict.

**High-priority payoff problem: u1 versus u7.** The opening promises “saved what was left of reality” followed by “made it worship him.” The payoff says “saved them” followed by “a throne above it.” Those are substantially the same information. A viewer who understood the hook gains little by reaching the ending. A more specific cost of the rescue, such as suppression of the inhabitants' former memories, would be a new revelation. This detail has publisher support, but it should be phrased as the comic's depiction rather than a general theory of Doom. [Marvel's official Secret Wars guide](https://www.marvel.com/articles/comics/secret-wars-official-marvel-guide) supports the worship/memory premise.

**u4 is expendable.** “Somewhere stranger than the world they had lost” delays the actual answer, Battleworld. Join the survival/arrival directly to the patchwork-planet reveal and spend the saved words on what that means for its people.

The episode should answer a specific question beyond “Was Doom controlling?” One workable chapter question is what the inhabitants lost when he saved them. The existing ending is a thematic restatement, not that answer.

### Part 2: the best distinctive incident is delayed

The hidden source and hamburger are the series' most memorable concrete material. **u2–u3 are the first likely attention dip:** another life-raft recap, then a general investigation. Get to the discoverable object/person sooner. Identify Miles as Spider-Man for a newcomer; his name alone does not establish why he belongs in this story.

**u4 overloads names.** “Beneath Doomstadt, they found Owen Reece: Molecule Man.” In one beat, a viewer receives a new place, a real name and an alias. “Beneath Doom's castle, they found Molecule Man” carries the necessary story. His other name can remain in notes.

**u8 abandons the most useful open question.** “When Reed Richards challenged the man on the throne” is less distinctive than the favor just established. Link the next chapter to a specific unresolved consequence. Do not imply that feeding Molecule Man caused Doom's defeat: Marvel treats his reward to Miles and the transfer of power to Reed as distinct developments. The [official Miles biography](https://www.marvel.com/characters/spider-man-miles-morales/in-comics) supports the food/debt/reward connection; [the Secret Wars guide](https://www.marvel.com/articles/comics/secret-wars-official-marvel-guide) supports the admission and power transfer.

### Part 3: its question is answered too early, then the ending diffuses

**u3 already pays off the title/hook.** Doom concedes Reed could have done better. The episode then adds rebuilding teams, Franklin, Miles's family and a moral. That is a list of aftermath facts after the promised answer. Keep the chain clear: admission, Molecule Man's decision, transfer of power, changed outcome. This is where fewer details would create a stronger resolution.

**u5 is a spoken-name bottleneck.** “Reed's family and the Future Foundation helped rebuild the multiverse, with Franklin and Molecule Man creating worlds.” Franklin is new and undefined, and the organization is still unexplained. Simplify the speech or establish why Franklin's identity matters. The detailed rebuilding claim is consistent with [Marvel's Future Foundation guide](https://www.marvel.com/comics/guides/2508/the-future-foundation); the issue is comprehension, not a proven contradiction.

**u6 has an unplanted emotional stake.** Miles's mother being alive again could be a powerful payoff, but no earlier episode establishes his loss. The viewer is asked to feel a restoration before being shown what was missing. Plant that loss with source support, or give the burger/return story its own short. Do not stretch this ending simply to include every notable consequence.

**u8 weakens the specific callback.** “Saving reality and owning it were never the same thing” announces the lesson after the food/favor result. End with a concrete earned consequence. If the contrast matters, let the two outcomes show it.

## Comics-fan check

The stated continuity is correctly restricted to the 2015 comic event; the drafts do not claim to predict the films. I did not establish a definite canon contradiction from the consulted recaps. That is a limited conclusion. Canonical Marvel pages returned HTTP403 on direct open; targeted search exposed official indexed text. I did not read the full issues or verify individual panels.

The main factual compression to guard is causation: the hamburger earns Miles gratitude; it should not become a claim that the burger itself defeated Doom. “Every territory answered to him” is a broad summary rather than a detailed description of how each region was administered. Retain that distinction if the visuals add specific laws, punishments, borders or examples that the script/source packet has not established.

## Rendering checks still required

There are no video timestamps in this review because no render was supplied. For a future export, I will check whether the opening shows a legible conflict immediately, whether the three chapters have distinguishable visual developments, whether names arrive faster than they can be understood, whether captions compete with diagrams, whether narration sounds like a list, and whether music/pauses merely inflate exposition. Those are pending checks, not reported defects in a video I have not watched.

Abstract code diagrams can explain the world and power transfer, but a succession of labels alone may feel like a presentation. Use a visible change tied to each beat: fragments becoming one world, an obscured power connection being revealed, the food gesture, then that connection changing hands. These are illustrative design suggestions, not a claim that comic panels or film assets have reuse permission.

Before calling the pilot ready, recheck the revised script against its actual chapter question and inspect the finished export. A technical pass and this critique cannot establish that real viewers will stay.
