# Audience script critique

Main assistant, skeptical audience pass; separate critic-agent run was unavailable. Original provided script and source brief only; no measured audience response or audio/video claim.

**Revise before render.**

- **u1 · major** — “India's exports got zero-duty access to New Zealand. Did India remove every tariff too?” The first line sounds universal before qualifying rules are mentioned. Put qualifying goods in the opening and ask a direction-specific question.
- **u2 · minor** — “The agreement took effect on October twentieth, twenty twenty-six. That date matters more than a dramatic headline.” The second sentence scolds headlines rather than progressing the explanation. Give the date in one sentence and show the two directions.
- **u4 · major** — “At launch, almost fifty-seven percent of New Zealand's exports to India became tariff-free. Further cuts are phased in.” A novice could interpret 57 percent as the share of tariff lines instead of export coverage. Attribute the figure to the NZ government and distinguish it explicitly.
- **u6 · minor** — “For your exam, a tariff is a border tax on imported goods. Removing it does not remove every other charge.” For your exam interrupts the core explanation, and the term arrives late. Define tariff at the first point it helps and distinguish customs duty from other costs.
- **u8 · major** — “So don't stop at the words free trade. Ask: which goods, which direction, and from what date?” The takeaway asks broad questions without putting the actual condition into a memorable rule. Close with direction and origin eligibility, tied back to this agreement.
