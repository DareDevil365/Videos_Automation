## What this pass establishes

The YouTube-domain search results inspected here expose dated, count-bearing examples in AI tools, studying, procrastination, confidence, job interviews, and earning-related content. Some examples have substantial reported lifetime reach; other videos with similar tool/earning promises have small counts. That supports testing these subjects and packaging approaches. It does **not** establish that any entire format is currently trending, automatically reproducible, profitable, accurate, or eligible for monetization.

All ten counts below were actually present in search results for the linked **native YouTube video page**. They are **indexed metadata**, not live counters independently read from the player. The index's last refresh time was not exposed. The date shown is the indexed publication date, not the research date. No video was watched, no audio or full transcript was inspected, and no editing, hook timing, duration, or exact shot structure was verified. A title containing `#shorts` is self-labeling; a watch URL or hashtag alone does not prove current Shorts classification.

The sample is a set of search-discovered cases, not a representative or randomized dataset. It includes older successes and lower-view counterexamples. No third-party analytics-site count, estimated earnings, audience geography, derived growth score, or private retention metric is used.

## Useful distinctions for this project

### Honest teaching versus high-claim packaging

E09 and E10 show that money-related promises can have very large public counts. They do not show that the underlying claims are false or true: labeling either creator a scammer from a title would be unsupported. E02 and E03 show that equally ambitious AI/earning language can coexist with low observed counts. Do not conclude that adding “free,” “AI,” “no investment,” or a large money figure reliably gets views.

For our channel, retain specificity and a visible payoff while making only claims the production record can support. A demonstrated invoice tracker or corrected job email is a reviewable result. An unperformed “I tried 100 tools” or invented weekly income would be fabricated experience. This is an editorial choice based on the user's truthful-production requirement; no experiment here proves it will outperform hype.

### Topic, packaging and production format are different evidence

The table primarily establishes **topic and title-promise examples with counts**. It does not establish whether these videos use gameplay, talking heads, stock footage, animated diagrams, fictional chats, captions, a podcast excerpt, or a particular musical track. Those details remain uninspected. We should not call these examples proof that “AI voice + generic background” works.

The title-level patterns worth testing are: one specific problem, one promised action or result, a before/after comparison, a defined question, or a clear cost constraint. These are proposed interpretations of the observed titles, not observed explanations of why the videos succeeded.

### Dates and audience matter

Most high-reach examples above were published in 2021–2024. They show accumulated reach, not a current 2026 growth rate. E01–E03 were published in 2026, but a single indexed snapshot cannot establish recent growth or momentum. None of these cases supports estimating US viewer share, India viewer share, RPM, sponsor income, affiliate conversions or net profit. Language, creator identity, title or subscriber counts cannot establish those quantities.

The choice of a large creator and a small creator also introduces selection bias. We have not collected all unsuccessful uploads, channel baselines, paid-promotion history, publication cadence, or audience overlap. No numeric “winning score,” average, or causal estimate is calculated from these cases.

## Next steps that resolve the actual uncertainty

1. **Inspect a small chosen reference set through normal permitted playback.** Record runtime, Shorts classification, opening promise, whether the promised result is delivered, visual recipe and any disclosures. Do not invent these observations if playback remains unavailable.
2. **Prepare three original sample Shorts:** one task-based free-AI example, one retrieval exercise, and one interview-answer repair. Save the source/claim record and the actual demonstration output; mark proposals and simulations clearly.
3. **Review production quality and policy/rights status separately from demand.** A popular source video does not make a derivative upload eligible or accurate. Missing proof or asset rights is a hold, not a popularity score.
4. **If publication is separately authorized, run a small channel-specific pilot** with comparable observation windows and materially distinct episodes. Inspect official owned-channel engaged views, viewing/retention measures and subscribers where available; do not estimate unavailable private competitor analytics.
5. **Make a continuation decision from our own results.** An isolated public hit is insufficient to select a niche or infer income. Keep low-result episodes in the experiment record rather than presenting only successes.
