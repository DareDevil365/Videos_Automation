## Recommendation and scope

These are viable format families to investigate, but “conspiracy” is not a single evidence category. Historical mysteries, fictional fan theories, clearly fictional secret plots, fact checks, speculation, and allegations about real people have different source requirements and risks. Likewise, an accurate chronology of a public disagreement differs from an invented exposé.

The proposed stronger first tests are a claim-versus-evidence mystery and an accurately sourced explanation of a public platform-rule disagreement. Keep the speculative sensational variants visible in the research backlog, with their added risks disclosed. This is an editorial feasibility recommendation, not proof of present demand or a decision on the user's behalf.

Gossip, chitchat and news feeds can supply topics and story leads; no original filming is required. The agents would do the research and draft original narration/visuals, rather than assigning article reading or recording to the user. A feed can establish that a rumor or opinion was posted without establishing that its underlying allegation is true. Researching a source and licensing footage are separate tasks: a script can explain an accurately attributed public claim using original graphics without copying that source's article, photographs or clips.

For each pop-culture story, distinguish a **confirmed event**, an **allegation/dispute**, an **unverified rumor**, a **fan theory**, **clearly fictional speculation**, and **opinion**. Accurately attribute who said what; attribution alone is neither verification nor permission to reuse their media. Rumor-retelling remains a higher-risk catalog variant, with the user's decision pending, rather than being silently removed or treated as automatically banned.

## E17 — Conspiracy / mystery claim versus evidence

- **Hook:** “This photo looks strange. What does the original record show?”
- **Structure:** identify the actual claim; show the relevant primary record; distinguish what it establishes from what remains unknown; provide a satisfying answer or an honest unresolved conclusion. A mystery does not need a fabricated final reveal.
- **Fact-based variant:** test a historical/scientific misconception with original diagrams, dated documents and clearly attributed quotations. The original contribution is selecting the precise claim, explaining the evidence and uncertainty, and creating comprehensible visuals.
- **Fiction/fan-theory variant:** label invented mysteries as fiction, or interpretation of a fictional work as a fan theory. Do not present invented documents, real-person statements or supposed scientific findings as authentic. Fictional source-work clips still need rights assessment.
- **Sensational speculative variant retained:** ominous footage, “what if” narration, a secret-plot premise or unresolved clues. This has higher risk of misleading certainty, unsupported real-person implications, manufactured evidence, sensational packaging, and weak advertiser fit. A question mark or tiny “theory” label does not determine the effect of the whole video. No specific accusation is proposed here.
- **Assets/source requirements:** identify the primary document and its date, provenance, what is actually visible, any contrary evidence, and the exact rights for each image/clip/music layer. Original maps and diagrams can reduce dependence on third-party footage. Public access to a document does not automatically license every included image.
- **Free automation fit:** medium for original script/diagram/narration assembly; lower for provenance, context and disputed evidence. The system can prepare a draft and source table, but must not turn an unanswered question into an asserted fact simply to finish the story.
- **Revenue/brand fit:** science/history/documentary-style work could be a fit for relevant brands or platform ads subject to actual eligibility; no sponsor or program is confirmed. Alarmist or harmful false claims can reduce ad suitability, distribution and brand trust. No CPM/RPM estimate is established.
- **Status:** researched format hypothesis; topic-specific facts/rights still need checking. Advisory preference: fact-based or explicitly fictional first. **User decision: pending.**

## E18 — Controversy chronology / competing claims

- **Hook:** “Three posts changed this debate. Here is what each one actually said.”
- **Structure:** date the initiating announcement; identify the criticism accurately; show the documented response; separate confirmed facts, disputed interpretations and unknowns; explain the practical question still open.
- **Fact-based variant:** a public policy/product/creative dispute, using original timelines and short precisely attributed quotations. Represent claims in proportion to evidence; “competing claims” does not mean every assertion deserves equal credibility. A company's statement proves what it said, not necessarily that the underlying claim is independently true.
- **Fictional variant:** original clearly labelled characters can dramatize a disagreement about made-up rules or misunderstandings. It should not look like leaked messages from a real person or a genuine news report.
- **Sensational variant retained:** personality-focused “exposed,” “destroyed,” or scandal framing with reaction cuts and music. Risks include implying guilt before facts exist, omitting context/responses, harassment, copying footage, stale-news mislabeling and adverse brand association. No invented allegation or target is supplied as an episode idea.
- **Gossip-feed retelling variant retained:** an entertainment/pop-culture feed supplies the lead and a new script retells the debate. A defensible description of what people are claiming still requires locating the actual statement and preserving uncertainty. A stronger variant explains how the rumor developed; a higher-risk variant repeats unverified claims for suspense. Neither the existence of a feed post nor the word “allegedly” determines truth, rights or platform eligibility. **User choice between these variants remains pending.**
- **Assets/source requirements:** primary dated statements from relevant parties, the full rule/document being debated, context before/after any quote, update/correction history, and separate clip/image/audio permissions. If a party's response is unavailable, say so; absence of a response is not proof of wrongdoing.
- **Free automation fit:** medium for document comparison and timeline graphics; lower for fast-moving disputes. Claims and chronology need rechecking before publication. A changing or unresolved factual record is not suited to a promise of unattended accuracy.
- **Revenue/brand fit:** audience-relevant product/platform explainers may suit relevant sponsorships or affiliates only where a real agreement exists. Do not assume a brand will sponsor criticism of itself or a competitor. General controversy does not guarantee high revenue.
- **Status:** researched format hypothesis. Advisory preference: narrow, source-backed disputes before personality exposés. **User decision: pending.**

## Official rules: what they do and do not say

### Misinformation

YouTube prohibits specified misleading/deceptive content with serious harm risk, including harmful manipulation or misattribution. Its documentation allows contextual/public-interest exceptions in some cases but says context is not a pass to promote misinformation. This is not a blanket prohibition on all conspiracy discussion, historical mystery or fictional theory. Separate medical/election policies can apply to specific claims; no such harmful claim is proposed in these concepts. [Official misinformation policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10834785?hl=en).

Practical design choice: attach a source and evidence status to every factual statement. Distinguish `documented`, `attributed_claim`, `interpretation`, `speculation`, `fiction` and `unknown`; those are editorial labels, not platform approval. A label must match the actual framing and cannot make a deceptive fabrication factual.

### Harassment, privacy and identity

YouTube restricts threats, doxxing, prolonged targeted insults and harmful impersonation, with stricter treatment for minors. Documentary context is assessed in context rather than guaranteed by a disclaimer. A controversy video should discuss substantiated public conduct or statements, not publish private contact details, fabricate messages or direct viewers to attack someone. [Harassment policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2802268?hl=en).

A real person's statement must be accurately sourced and quoted. Do not manufacture an accusation or deceptive voice/likeness to make a debate more dramatic. Potential reputational/legal exposure remains distinct from platform enforcement; this report does not make a jurisdiction-specific legal finding.

### Ad suitability and originality

YouTube's advertiser rules apply to titles, thumbnails, tags, descriptions and the video. Its defined “controversial issues” category covers specific sensitive subjects; it does not mean every online disagreement is automatically barred from ads. Context and treatment can matter, including documentary/news discussion of sensitive events. Graphic, exploitative, hateful or misleading treatment can affect eligibility even without a copyright problem. [Advertiser-friendly guidelines](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278?hl=en).

Copyright clearance, source truth and originality remain separate checks. A narrated rumor over reused unrelated footage does not establish a meaningful original investigation. Conversely, the use of AI-assisted diagrams does not by itself prove ineligibility. [Monetization policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en).

## Proceeding without turning speculation into fabricated reporting

1. Keep E17 and E18, including sensational variants, in the visible catalog. Record the user's chosen risk posture separately from factual source permissions and platform rules; no risk is recorded as accepted yet.
2. Build a claim ledger before script generation: exact claim, original source/date, what it supports, contrary context, unresolved question and whether it concerns a real person/event. An original source can itself be mistaken or self-interested; attribute it appropriately rather than equating “primary” with “true.”
3. Start with EB20 or EB21 for an evidence graphic, and EB22 or EB23 for a timeline/skit. This tests source fidelity and a complete payoff using inexpensive original visuals; it is not a forecast of views.
4. For real disputes, recheck primary records immediately before publication and maintain a correction/update path. Do not recycle old footage as breaking news, invent responses, manufacture engagement, or make a current allegation from an unrelated clip.
5. Treat narration, clip selection, title/thumbnail and music as parts of the same assertion. A careful script can still mislead if the packaging implies a cover-up or verdict the evidence does not establish.
