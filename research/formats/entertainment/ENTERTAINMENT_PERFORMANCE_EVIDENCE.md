## What the evidence actually supports

There are real, count-bearing public examples of sigma/caption edits, chat/comeback content, storytime/gameplay titles, podcast promotion, and movie recommendations. The strongest native search-indexed examples found were a celebrity sigma edit with **66,619,241** indexed views and a Beluga comeback video with **10,012,982** indexed views. Other examples had **61**, **455**, or **14,359** indexed views. These examples show that individual videos can attract very different outcomes; they do not establish a dependable winning formula.

The most important limitations are recency and inspection. Most count-bearing examples found are old. Native video-page fetches were frequently throttled, so the numbers below come from search-indexed metadata associated with exact YouTube URLs, not a live signed-in counter. No video was watched frame by frame in this pass. A title suggesting a format does not verify a particular split-screen layout, original contribution, music track, story quality, rights position, or cause of its views.

The exact **unrelated creator clip + gameplay split-screen**, **poster/GIF + one-word movie verdict**, and **nine-movie list + viewer supplies the tenth** variants remain insufficiently evidenced here. Adjacent examples exist; they must not be presented as proof of the precise variants. This gap is not proof those formats fail or do not exist.

## Evidence method

- Used bounded public web searches and native YouTube/video-page references; secondary indexes were used only to discover titles/URLs. Their estimated earnings, “viral scores,” growth claims and inferred audience locations were not adopted.
- **S = native search-indexed metadata.** The title, uploader, description, publication date or count was returned for the exact YouTube URL. Retrieval date is 2026-11-01; source indexing/counter timestamp is not supplied, so counts may be stale.
- **P = primary post/source page.** Useful for confirming a published reference or campaign exists, but not necessarily for its YouTube performance.
- **Lead = exact native URL discovered elsewhere, native body unavailable.** No count is adopted solely from that secondary page.
- A `watch?v=` URL does not by itself establish Shorts classification. A title containing `#shorts` is a label, not an inspected duration/aspect ratio. Do not compare an unknown-duration or long-form video to a 30-second Short as if they were identical products.
- Titles below are identifiers. Narrative/edit observations are explicitly limited to what the readable title/description establishes.

## Why these examples cannot tell us expected views or income

This is a purposive discovery sample, not a representative census of uploads or a controlled experiment. It contains older videos with years of exposure, established channels, uncertain duration, and both small/large counters. Search ranking also favors discoverable examples. It omits many failures and inaccessible posts. Therefore it cannot establish average views, a success probability, which edit caused performance, a statistically best niche, or a current growth rate.

No rights/YPP approval, sponsorship agreement, RPM, retention, engaged views, impressions, click-through rate, repeat-viewer behavior, or creator earnings were available. Raw public views are not interchangeable with qualified monetized views. Nothing in the sample establishes eligibility to reuse a video. The [YouTube monetization rules](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en) still apply separately.

Some secondary sites returned different numbers for the same titles. Those differences were not reconciled into a fabricated “live” count; only exact native-search metadata is reported in the main table. The index can be stale, and a counter can change after retrieval. Do not use this report's numbers in public claims about creators' present earnings or growth.

## US-content fit is a hypothesis, not observed audience data

An English original chat/story or a recommendation framed around an internationally understandable viewing preference could be tested for a US audience. Familiar language, comprehensible references, pacing and availability-relevant context may help the content fit that audience; none guarantees distribution or pay. The celebrity examples here are not evidence for a US audience strategy. Do not invent a US identity or label a channel as US-operated to target US viewers.

For an India-operation scenario, publishing eligibility and payout onboarding depend on the actual destination/account rules. Worldwide audience targeting does not change those creator-side requirements. TikTok is outside the India baseline described in the separate revenue research.

## How to proceed

1. Keep all entertainment families in the research catalog, including higher-risk variants. Ask the user to choose risk tradeoffs from explicit evidence; no choice is presumed here.
2. Select three distinct private production samples: an original chat comeback, a complete original storytime, and either an original simulation joke or a researched movie recommendation with eligible assets. A source-dependent creator edit remains a parallel sample option once an exact source is usable. This is a production-fit recommendation, not a performance ranking.
3. For any format promoted as a current-view opportunity, collect a balanced recent reference set: several exact episodes from more than one channel, dates/durations, native count snapshots, and directly inspected layouts where accessible. Include low outcomes as well as prominent examples. Do not invent a dataset if the pages are inaccessible.
4. Separate the variables being tested: story premise, hook, narrative/edit structure, background, voice, cover/title and language. Avoid simultaneous wholesale changes that make lessons uninterpretable. No automatic duplicate upload or spam experiment is proposed.
5. Following separately authorized publication, use owned-channel reports at comparable observation windows and mark missing data as missing. Distinguish technical feasibility, audience response and monetization decisions. Changes are hypotheses until enough relevant observations support them.

Current deliverable: **10 exact native-video references, 9 with native search-indexed counts, covering 6 broad title/description families**. Exact split-screen and minimal movie/nine-tenth layouts remain evidence gaps. No claims of current trend leadership, typical views, causal editing effects or earnings are supported by this pass.
