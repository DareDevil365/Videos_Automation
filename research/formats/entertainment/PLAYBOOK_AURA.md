# Aura / sigma edit playbook — evidence and implementation brief

Research date: 2 November 2026. This is a public-player **frame audit of 13 references**, not a claim to have fully watched or listened to them. Opening, midpoint and ending frames were inspected using an isolated public in-app browser tab, followed by an unsigned-in Chromium session for the final two references. Sound remained muted. Exact songs, speech speed, beat timing, cut frequency and private retention were not assessed. The paired [inventory](aura_reference_inventory.json) records per-reference timestamps and observations.

## What the reference sample actually supports

Aura has several distinct visual grammars. Most sampled references use a source event, confrontation, comparison or reaction that makes the final image meaningful. They do not simply praise a creator over a repeated portrait. None of the inspected samples showed a gameplay lower panel. A gameplay split remains the user's requested production variant to test; it is not a demonstrated winning layout from this sample.

The requested early-days-to-success montage is also a separate **production hypothesis**. This sample supports varied source scenes and comparison payoffs, but does not establish that a creator wealth collage will outperform an event edit. Visible luxury objects do not prove ownership, income, a relationship, or a person's life story.

Public view totals below are rounded display values recorded during this audit. They are an attention signal, not a promise that copying a format reproduces its performance. Search ranking, creator audience, subject familiarity, source context, distribution and survivorship all confound comparison.

## Family A: event / reaction reversal

Use a permitted source event with enough context to understand the reaction. Three distinct shots can be ranges from one source file; requiring four separate files here would be artificial.

1. Open with the action, expression or situation that creates a simple question. A brief caption supplies missing context.
2. Show the challenge or comparison. Preserve enough context to avoid inventing a meaning.
3. Land on an actual response, composed reaction, unexpected arrival or decisive action.
4. Hold the reward frame long enough to understand it. A short status graphic can punctuate it.

References: Speed-at-end, Slipz, car arrival, Weak Hero and the short social encounter. This family depends on comprehensible action. The title alone cannot create a payoff from an unrelated face.

Proposed production target: 12–24 seconds, 4–7 meaningful ranges, one pivot, one payoff. These are starting settings, not measured successful-shot averages. Do not claim a cut every two seconds was verified from three sampled frames.

## Family B: progression montage

This implements the user's requested early-days / aura-minus to stronger-present-day / aura-plus direction. It must contain **at least four distinct meaningful images or clip ranges**, preferably six shots. The first, pivot and last shots must differ in information, not merely crop.

Suggested sequence: modest or awkward but accurately described setup → challenge → clear turn → visible achievement or impressive action → escalation → strongest supported image. An achievement needs a source fact if the caption asserts it. A photograph near a sports car is not proof of ownership.

For a real creator, use verified source images and factual neutral captions. Do not manufacture a girlfriend, mansion, cash holdings or brand endorsement. For an original fictional character, a clearly fictional glow-up can use invented props and outcomes. Decorative aura scores are humorous editorial graphics, never financial measurements.

Proposed production target: 15–25 seconds, captions around 1–5 words, music-only by default, optional brief narration when context requires it. Avoid reusing one portrait as every shot; crop or zoom does not make it a distinct asset.

## Family C: comparison / cinematic performance

A source scene supplies the visual analogy: cold versus fiery character, two brands, or several dramatic character entrances. Use an accurate short premise and a legible turn, then the actual visible response. Do not turn a joke into a false factual superiority claim.

The animation and film references demonstrate framing and progression, not permission to reuse studio footage. Their public availability or view count does not establish a license.

## Editor template requirements

- Canvas: 1080×1920. Variants: full frame, or upper subject panel + narrow caption/status band + lower **gameplay** panel. No ASMR lower panel for aura.
- The upper subject must have multiple informative shots. Face-aware crops should retain expressions and avoid covering relevant actions.
- Caption band: short premise / pivot / payoff, high contrast, safe from YouTube controls. Never a full paragraph of creator praise.
- Per-shot effects are optional: punch-in at pivot, brief flash at a change, restrained shake on impact, and glow on the final reward. The scene should still communicate with effects disabled.
- Coins, particles, plus/minus indicators and aura gauges may reinforce an already understandable outcome. They must not stand in for missing evidence.
- Permit one final hold; prohibit a timeline consisting entirely of repeated zoom-in/zoom-out on one picture.
- Every shot binds a specific asset ID and, for a clip, explicit in/out times. Never fill the whole project from a single generic portrait fallback.
- Bind music from the registered, eligible asset library. If a suitable track is missing, return `needs_music_asset`; do not silently substitute the rejected synthetic loop.
- An event may use one video with several meaningful ranges. A progression photo montage requires at least four distinct asset identities. Check both asset diversity and semantic diversity.

## Music brief — proposed, not an audio finding

The user requests phonk / funk-like energy. The inspected title of the fantasy comparison names X-cool (ultra slowed), and the superhero title names Funk Sigilo (Slowed); neither track was listened to or licensed in this audit. Do not download or reuse either on that basis.

For this template, request a licensed eligible track with a clear low-energy intro, identifiable change/drop, strong accent opportunities and no distracting vocal conflict. Record track ID, source, license, platform restrictions, attribution and pivot timestamp. Place the visual turn near that timestamp, then check the exported video by ear. A title saying copyright free and a creator not usually claiming content are insufficient rights evidence.

YouTube's in-product Shorts audio selection and an externally rendered music file are different workflows. This template should not assume that a song offered inside Shorts can be downloaded and mixed into a reusable file for every platform.

## Gemini creative prompt contract

Provide the model an actual asset catalog and a choice of `event_reaction`, `progression` or `comparison`. The creative task is to choose a coherent setup, turn and earned visual payoff using those assets. Do not ask it to invent facts or pretend unavailable assets exist.

Required response fields:

- `variant`, `premise`, `target_duration_seconds`, `payoff_description`.
- `units`: stable ID, exact caption/narration if any, purpose (setup/challenge/pivot/escalation/payoff), supported fact IDs, and a concrete visible action or image requirement.
- `music_brief`: mood, energy curve and desired pivot location. This is a request, not a claim that a suitable asset exists.
- `engagement_question`: optional authentic comparison question after the payoff. No claim of a prize, fake urgency, invented viewer consensus or withholding the promised result.

Add these hard prompt instructions:

> Use only provided facts for real people. Give the viewer a visible payoff. Every new unit must add information. Do not repeat a portrait with cosmetic crops. Do not fabricate ownership, earnings, relationships or endorsements. Keep decorative scores explicitly humorous. If source context or the payoff asset is missing, return a missing requirement rather than inventing it.

## Gemini director prompt contract

The director receives the approved creative units plus available editor capabilities and registered assets. It preserves unit IDs and exact spoken/caption words. It may choose cuts, asset ranges, crop, layout, short effects and timing; it may not rewrite the script or create unsupported biographical claims.

Return a timeline whose shots explicitly identify `unit_id`, `asset_id`, `source_in` / `source_out` when applicable, `duration`, `layout`, `effect`, caption and purpose. Also return `music_asset_id`, a checked drop timestamp and unresolved needs. The renderer should reject missing assets or missing appropriate music rather than presenting a weak fallback as a finished aura video.

Asset availability is a production constraint. A research title or URL is not an asset permission record, and a model-generated search query is not a downloaded usable file.

## Acceptance and refinement

Before a publishable prototype: verify the opening makes sense without knowing the creator; setup and payoff are different; every caption matches visible/source-supported information; the final image resolves the setup; gameplay does not obscure the primary action; music is eligible and audited by ear; no missing-asset fallback remains.

For testing, compare event/reaction against progression and full frame against gameplay split. Change one main variable at a time. Measure viewed-versus-swiped, average view duration, retention curve, rewatches and genuine comments using the channel's own analytics once available. Public view counts cannot tell us these numbers.

## Current implementation status and limits

This document and its inventory are research artifacts. Root integration owns the Gemini writer/director, shot validation, editor and production changes. The contracts above are a proposed implementation brief, not a statement that all checks already run.

The evidence gap is explicit: this pass inspected three public frames per reference. A complete audio/timing audit and actual production asset permissions still remain. It would be inaccurate to describe this as 13 complete audiovisual watches or a proven recipe for retention.
