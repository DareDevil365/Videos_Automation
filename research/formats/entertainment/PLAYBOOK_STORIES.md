# Story formats: evidence-led playbooks

Research date: 2 November 2026. First evidence pass; genre coverage remains incomplete. This file distinguishes observed public-video frames, readable text, metadata and design hypotheses. Public view counts are observations, not retention data or proof that a technique caused success. Reference media are research material, not cleared production inputs.

## Current user direction

- Horror/mystery: 30-45 seconds when the story requires it, female narration, slower tension-building delivery, explicit pauses and a completed reveal. Use relevant usable stock photographs/clips rather than generic procedural drawings. A dark elevator, corridor and phone can be literal assets; do not fabricate readable screen details unless needed.
- Relationship/tea storytime: quicker delivery, original fictional social conflict, clear identities, escalating evidence and a complete outcome. Smooth ASMR can be a decorative lower region or full-background layer. Its timing need not depict the narration, but the primary story layer must.
- Ensemble cutouts: a cast bible, stable faces/costumes/voices, an establishing group frame and active-speaker closeups made by hiding others and changing scale/position. William Osman's supplied reference is inspiration for collage grammar, not permission for copying TV bodies or celebrity performances.
- Female narration is a user-selected creative hypothesis. This review does not establish that women are universally perceived as safer or that a voice gender improves retention.

### What these samples support

Several independently sampled chat references use a **compact upper overlay over full-canvas background activity**. A compulsory equal split is therefore a supported product option, not the only evidence-matched default. The actual user reference uses real-world moving footage, so do not silently substitute generated gameplay. Messages are the narrative; satisfying footage is a decorative continuity layer. Keep speaker identity stable and show the most recent conversation without an unreadable wall.

The black-background reference is a useful alternative when the conversation itself needs most of the screen. It demonstrates that gameplay is optional, not that removing it improves performance. Partial frames establish visible reveal devices, not whether the entire story paid off; our complete-ending requirement comes from explicit user feedback.

### Horror-specific findings from the expanded frame audit

There are two distinct observed families: scene-based fiction and host commentary with inserts. They should not share one blindly copied template. Our requested fiction pilot fits the first family. The high-reach real-estate and Father's Day examples establish that portrait/monster changes and simple animated settings can accompany large accumulated reach; they do not prove that female narration, a particular beat rate or a specific song produced that reach.

The closer low-view hospital and mirror examples are useful **feasibility/grammar** references: location setup → contrasting situation/evidence → literal clue or answer. Their small recent counts also prevent treating the surface layout as a guaranteed viral formula. A dated photograph or contradictory text message is a semantic insert. When the story mentions that evidence, give it the screen, then return to the character/location. A decorative gameplay/ASMR layer is optional; none was visible in the sampled horror scenes. Do not force aura music onto this template.

The additional footage example also shows why successful-looking packaging should not be copied uncritically: inconsistent time details between title/caption and a literal bright-elevator/darkness mismatch are visible. Add a continuity check over named times, identities, places and prop states. Require semantic shot plans to describe what can actually be seen. A known cue should be demonstrated with a matching insert rather than illustrated by an unrelated or contradictory image. These are quality-control implications, not claims about how that reference's viewers responded.

The mirror sample makes an identifiable answer visible near the end. This supports testing an explicit payoff frame. It does not justify copying that mirror story. Our writer must create a different original mystery with its own planted clue/reveal connection.

## Design rules to implement, subject to reference refinement

### A. A horror Short must resolve its own question

The draft must name its opening anomaly, plant two observable clues and deliver a final fact/reveal that changes the meaning of those clues. Ending only with a threat such as “do not turn around” creates an open setup; it is not automatically a payoff. A final sting can follow the answer, but cannot replace it. An unresolved question is acceptable only when it is the explicit format promise, rather than a missing story ending.

Working 40-second beat budget: 0-3s concrete anomaly; 3-10s orientation and stakes; 10-23s two escalating discoveries; 23-31s apparent explanation/counter-clue; 31-38s reveal that connects to the opening; 38-42s short aftershock or a genuine interpretation question. These are proposed editing budgets, not measured universal best-performing timings.

Narration starting hypothesis: 125-150 spoken words/minute with about 2-4 seconds of deliberate total silence distributed across scene turns. At 40s, around 75-90 spoken words is a useful first drafting range. Measure the actual synthesized track, since voice engine speed, punctuation and pauses make word-count estimates imperfect. Leave a short pause immediately before the reveal, then keep the answer intelligible; do not whisper so quietly that the phone speaker loses words.

### B. Tea/texting storytime should have a complete social reversal

Start on the suspicious message or impossible relationship detail. Introduce no more than the necessary two or three relationships at once. Escalate with distinct new evidence, not several paraphrases of the same accusation. Let the ending settle what happened and what the character decided. The audience can debate the decision after the outcome exists.

The actual Narrated Texts sample supports readable conversational bubbles over a continuous satisfying clip. It does not prove the same background or story should be copied. Use original fiction and reusable permitted ASMR. Keep recent messages visible long enough to read; ordinary response messages can move briskly, while the evidence/reveal deserves more dwell time. Starting hypothesis: 165-190 spoken words/minute, calibrated against actual audio and text reading time.

### C. Cutout scenes require a director plan, not another script

Stage 1 creates the exact spoken units. Stage 2 returns a cast and shot plan referencing those unit IDs without changing, summarizing or adding dialogue. The voice stage reads the original units verbatim. The director stage can choose only supported composition modes and expressions. Fixed faces/costumes and voice IDs should persist for one character throughout the episode.

For a four-person exchange, start with one establishing shot. Each following line selects the active speaker and reuses the same background. A closeup can be implemented by hiding other actors and positioning/scaling the speaker until face and shoulders fill the visual region. A reaction cut can precede the punchline, but must not obscure who said a line. The narrator and characters have separate voice assignments.

## Proposed Gemini contract

Use two bounded calls rather than ask an unrestricted model to author executable edit instructions:

1. **Writer:** produce original fictional script units, hook/question, clue IDs, exact payoff unit and optional comment question. Specify format, target duration and voice pace. Validate word count and the actual audio duration; one bounded rewrite can fix an under-length or missing-payoff draft.
2. **Director:** input the frozen script units and supported scene/asset/voice inventory. Output cast assignments, per-unit shot requirements, actor visibility/positions, active speaker, background query, sound cue and explicit pause durations. Every unit ID appears exactly once in order. It must not alter the script text.

Every asset request carries `purpose`, literal `query`, `kind`, `needed_seconds`, crop requirements and whether it is semantic or decorative. A direct URI or file path is resolved by code after acquisition; the model must not invent one. A missing usable background or face holds the job rather than rendering an unrelated file.

## Sound starting points

Horror: low sustained texture, sparse environmental sounds, a pause/drop before the reveal and a small resolved sting afterwards. Tea/comedy: subtle message arrivals and restrained rhythmic bed or no music. Never let the bed compete with the spoken consonants. A horror bed is not interchangeable with phonk. Music mood and rights are separate requirements; no current public song is cleared by this playbook.

## Bounded writer and director prompts

These are editorial requirements to encode in Gemini prompts, not instructions to copy a reference story. Existing implementation may represent them with a stricter JSON schema; keep one canonical capability list in code.

**Writer system/request additions:**

> Write an original fictional short, not a retelling of a reference script. Return only the supplied script schema. Use short spoken units with stable IDs and exact speaker names. Start with a specific situation the viewer can picture. Every escalation must add a different fact. Identify the opening question, planted clue IDs and the exact final payoff unit. Deliver the promised answer or concrete reversal before an optional genuine question. Do not label a vague threat as a payoff. No false real-world accusation or fake factual evidence. Do not request an asset URL or invent a local file. Produce dialogue only; a separate director preserves it verbatim.

Format-specific additions:

- **horror_stock:** 30-45s target; one impossible detail, orientation, two meaningful clues, explanation/reversal connecting to those clues, short aftershock. Start drafting around75-90 words for a40s concept, then fit against measured synthesized audio. Female narrator; restrained pace, no shouted generic stop-scrolling line. Pauses must leave room for the answer. Suggested pause0.25-0.7s around a discovery/reveal; suggested total deliberate silence2-4s. These are user-aligned hypotheses, not measured reference values.
- **tea_chat:** 30-45s target; suspicious message, who is involved, new contradictory evidence, social reveal, decision. Usually2-3 active relationships and roughly90-125 words as a drafting range. A recurring narrator is optional; exact dialogue belongs to named speakers. Keep messages one sentence or short thought. End with a debatable decision after the truth is revealed, not an unfinished accusation.
- **cutout_ensemble:** 30-50s target;2-4 recurring fictional characters plus optional narrator. Every line has an explicit speaker. Hook with conflict, show a reaction, escalate through meaningful dialogue, finish with a concrete funny/social reversal. Keep staging achievable with static cutouts. Different voices are character assignments, not imitations of identifiable people.

**Director system/request additions:**

> DIRECTOR ONLY. Input script units are immutable. Preserve every ID, speaker, exact text and order. First check whether the promised payoff exists in those words; hold for writer revision if absent. Do not repair the story by adding dialogue. Assign a consistent cast identity, avatar/animal choice, costume and allowed voice to each named speaker. Plan one supported scene per unit. Use only supported framing/motion/sound enums. Use exact registered asset IDs or literal search queries. No fabricated URLs, paths, screenshots or executable commands. Reuse backgrounds for location continuity. A character closeup hides others and enlarges the focused actor; do not request rigging, walking, complex acting or lip-sync. Actual timestamps come from measured TTS, not guessed seconds. Return only the supplied director schema.

The director needs both `background_query` (where the scene is) and `visual_query` (what visible object/action the spoken line means). For example, a phone clue needs a phone-in-hand insert, not another unrelated elevator shot. Semantic backgrounds/inserts must change as the story changes. A decorative ASMR layer can continue across story cuts. Requesting new images every2s is not the same as relevance: the editor should hold an important clue long enough to see it.

### Minimal editor grammar

| Template | Supported grammar | Asset requirement / failure condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| story_stock | Literal background or insert; restrained crop/slow push; small captions; occasional pause/hold before reveal | Matching usable elevator/corridor/phone/etc. image or clip for each semantic beat. Hold if absent rather than drawing a generic replacement. |
| chat_asmr | Continuous owned/permitted decorative footage; compact upper card or deliberate split; short gray/blue bubbles; reveal only current/new states | Stable contact header and sides; layout must stay readable on a phone. Reveal dwell should accommodate spoken line and text reading. |
| cutout_cast | One establishing group frame; fixed stage slots; medium speaker shot or isolated shoulder/head closeup; one reaction; shared location until movement | Register one consistent cutout per actor and allowed expression variants. No arbitrary celebrity heads or TV bodies reused from the reference. |

For cutout scenes, program-defined positions should take priority over unrestricted model coordinates: wide = cast slots; medium = speaker plus one listener; closeup = only focused actor. Keep all character heads/captions within the safe picture region. If an actor changes, do not remap the previous actor's voice. A narrator can be off screen. Do not put the entire dialogue in a separate headline bar and then duplicate it again as subtitles.

### Validation and refinement gates

1. Structural checks: every unit exactly once, original text unchanged, known speaker IDs, valid asset IDs, allowed edit actions, bounded pause values.
2. Editorial review: explicit opening promise, clue-to-reveal connection, final outcome visible in actual words; review is model judgement, not objective proof of quality.
3. Timing: synthesize first, total the measured speech + pauses + breathing room. Revise the writer if duration is too short/long. A word-count pass alone cannot certify duration:105 words at125WPM already takes50.4s before pauses.
4. Visual review: inspect hook/clue/reveal frames and a complete rendered prototype; semantic visuals match narration; decorative footage stays subordinate. Check phone-sized readability and speaker continuity.
5. Sound review: listen to the actual final mix, check intelligibility and silence/pause placement. This research has not performed an audio audit; the future prototype review must.
6. Pilot: compare completion/average view duration, swipe behavior and genuine comments on owned-channel analytics. Treat small samples and algorithmic distribution cautiously; change one major creative choice at a time when practical.
