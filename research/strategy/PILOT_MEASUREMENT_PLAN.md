# Two-channel Shorts pilot and measurement plan

Prepared: 2026-11-01. **Proposed execution plan, not an implemented application or scheduled publishing run.** Scope: two Shorts channels, US-first audience testing, India-operated account/payment setup, free tools/APIs, and no requirement for the user to film, write scripts, record gameplay, or edit.

This document connects the existing format catalogs, risk register and revenue reports. It does not change the user's pending format/risk choices, clear third-party assets, accept campaign terms or authorize publication. Operational timing below is a planning assumption; source access, account readiness and a working production pipeline can move it later.

## What the pilot must establish

1. Which distinct formats can be produced repeatedly with available free tools and acceptable source conditions.
2. Whether our own viewers choose to continue watching, understand the payoff and ask for related content.
3. Whether the audience actually includes the intended US viewers, once that report is available.
4. Whether the production and publishing process is reliable and traceable with minimal owner involvement.
5. Whether any revenue mechanism has become genuinely accessible, approved and payable. Views alone do not establish this.

A high-view public example demonstrates an outcome for that specific upload. It does not establish a typical result, a monetized channel, a permitted asset library or the causal effect of gameplay, music, captions or timing. Existing-channel size, audience, source celebrity, distribution and upload age can differ. Our first 18 uploads are a feasibility and learning sample, not a reliable income prediction or a statistically established format ranking.

## Stage 0: research and production gates

The researcher can prepare scripts, factual sources, acquisition routes and source evidence. The editor can render original text/graphics/animation and run software demonstrations using synthetic data. The account owner still needs to control login/OAuth consent, identity/payment verification, final policy decisions and any contractual commitment. Those steps require authentic owner details; agents cannot invent them or move residence by selecting a foreign audience. The project should record only completion/status, not identity documents or bank details in research files.

| Gate | Concrete evidence required | If incomplete |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Owner choices | Selected formats, source strategy, outstanding risk choices linked to the decision ledger | Research remains visible; do not treat silence as permission for a consequential action |
| Rights and source | Exact assets, owner/recording rights, music/game/font rights, acquisition route, scope/expiry, commercial/edit rights and campaign conditions | Keep `rights_pending`; offer a specific original/generated alternative as an option |
| Factual and editorial | Education claims linked to primary sources; actual demo outputs retained; fiction/dramatization identified; complete payoff, accurate context and packaging | Revise or keep in research; no fabricated earnings, private messages or first-person experiments |
| Technical render | Playable vertical render; audible intelligible narration; readable captions; synchronized audio; no blank frames, clipped text, wrong version or accidental personal data | Return to the failed production step; preserve completed upstream work |
| Source/platform policy | Specific review of reused/inauthentic content, community/ad suitability, audience designation, synthetic-content disclosure and commercial relationship | Report the issue separately from the user's business-risk preference; no general “safe” badge |
| Account/publication | Correct channel ID, authorized account scope, intended visibility and schedule, unique job/upload IDs, a working private upload/recovery test | Stay local/private; no duplicate public upload on an uncertain retry |
| Revenue/payment | Actual program eligibility, accepted modules/terms, campaign approval state, budget/thresholds and payout onboarding | Record revenue access as unverified; do not book forecast money as earned |

Private test copies establish rendering/processing and account correctness, not public demand. Content and packaging remain subject to separate platform rules. Private-preview/review gates are project design choices; this plan does not claim YouTube requires manual approval before every post. [Monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en), [advertiser guidelines](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278?hl=en).

## Evidence protocol for public references

For each selected format, inspect several recent examples and at least one less spectacular result where accessible, rather than collecting only viral hits. This is a convenience sample for creative research, not a representative dataset. Include relevant US-English examples without assuming their audience geography is visible.

For every inspected example record:

- Canonical URL, channel, publication date, observation time, duration and format ID.
- Public count and exactly how/where it was observed; `unavailable` if it could not be verified. Never invent missing views or label a search snippet as a fresh on-page count.
- Hook, setup, edit pattern, original contribution and payoff in the researcher's own words.
- What the example supports: format existence, a particular public outcome, a creative reference or a source lead.
- What it does not establish: earnings, YPP status, retention, paid distribution, permission, typical success or our production feasibility.

Store source-research caches according to the applicable API/service retention rules. Do not compute a new API-derived viral score, engagement ratio or earnings estimate without the relevant permission. Keep creator permission evidence separately from transient statistics. [YouTube developer policies](https://developers.google.com/youtube/terms/developer-policies), [additional analytics rules](https://developers.google.com/youtube/terms/derived-metrics-policy).

## Which metrics mean what now

Since March 31, 2025, Shorts public views count starts/replays without a minimum watch duration. The previous Shorts measure remains available as engaged views. A high raw view count therefore is not proof that viewers watched the story or generated earnings. [Shorts view-count guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10059070?hl=en).

YouTube's newer explanation says views across formats were standardized from the first frame on August 24, 2026. Engaged views involve a click-to-watch or watching past the initial seconds; core watch-quality analytics remain anchored to engagement rather than merely starting playback. Use current qualified/eligible metrics from the Earn/monetization reports for eligibility and earnings instead of treating public views as interchangeable. [Official engaged-view explanation](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/engaged-views-youtube-explained/).

| Record from our own official reports, when available | How to use it | Important limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Raw views | Describes exposure/play starts | Includes starts/replays; not earnings or completed viewing |
| Engaged views | Evidence of viewers continuing/clicking into viewing | Not a guarantee of a full view or YPP-eligible/revenue-generating view |
| Official “Stayed to watch” / viewed versus swiped report | Inspect whether the opening encourages people to continue | Use the platform-reported value; do not recalculate a replacement API ratio |
| Average view duration and average percentage viewed | Inspect watch quality among viewers who stayed | Compare similar lengths and report definitions; not a universal success threshold |
| Retention curve / detected moments, if available | Locate confusing setup, slow section or premature exit | Short pilot videos may not receive detected key-moment labels; missing labels are not a failure |
| Likes, shares, comments and subscribers gained/lost | Separate supporting evidence and qualitative audience questions | Counts are not quality proof; no fabricated engagement, incentives or new composite score |
| Traffic sources | Understand Shorts Feed versus search/channel/external discovery | Distribution differs between videos; do not compare as though exposure were controlled |
| Viewer geography | Test the US-first audience hypothesis | Report availability and coverage, not “zero US viewers” from a blank row |
| Estimated revenue / monetization state | Track official reported earnings once accessible | Estimated is not settled cash; use its actual metric/time window and currency |

Official content-performance definitions distinguish Shorts engagement metrics and allow Advanced Mode comparison/export. Not every Studio field exists in every API report; do not substitute an unsupported API field or scrape the interface. [YouTube content performance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281?hl=en), [authorized channel reports](https://developers.google.com/youtube/analytics/channel_reports).

Retention information normally takes one to two days to process. Detected key moments additionally have length/view conditions: the official guide says at least 60 seconds and 100 views. Many 25–45-second pilot Shorts will not qualify for those labels; use available metrics and playback review instead. [Retention reporting](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415?hl=en).

Geography and demographic data can be withheld for limited traffic, and the threshold is unpublished. Geography is unavailable in realtime reports and for private/unlisted videos. Missing or suppressed rows are `not_available`/`limited`, never inferred zero. Report totals need not equal the sum of visible rows. [Limited-data guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9101241?hl=en).

## Episode ledger and observation windows

Each job needs one record connecting creative choices to evidence:

```text
channel_id, job_id, format_id, episode_id, script_version, render_version
story_or_claim_sources, rights_manifest, disclosures, risk_decision_links
duration_seconds, language, intended_audience, hook_variant, payoff_summary
production_elapsed, retry_reason, free_provider_usage, source_expiry
published_video_id, published_at_utc, planned_slot, actual_slot
review_target: 24h | 72h | 7d
observed_at_utc, report_window_start, report_window_end, report_timezone
window_kind: elapsed_snapshot | calendar_day_report
metric_name, official_value, source_report, data_status
geography_report_status, actual_visible_country_rows
production_notes, audience_questions, next_hypothesis
```

The observation ledger is a proposed data contract, not an installed database. Record target and actual observation times; if the machine was offline, write the delay. A calendar-day Analytics query is not an exact 24-hour snapshot. Compare like windows and data states; do not pretend the most recent video has a full seven-day history.

Use 24-hour, 72-hour and seven-day check-ins for **each** upload:

1. **24 hours:** verify publication/processing, claims/disclosures, correct channel and usable audio/captions. Capture available official results; label provisional or missing data. Do not declare a winner.
2. **72 hours:** capture the same report definitions, inspect available engagement/retention and qualitative comments, check whether the hook and payoff appear to match. Separate actual observation from an explanation we hypothesize.
3. **Seven days:** compare completed episodes within the same format/duration group and note source/distribution differences. Write `continue`, `revise`, `hold for evidence`, or `stop this source`, with a concrete reason and the next small experiment.

Keep title/description/edit changes in a version log. Prefer making the intended revision in a new episode so the earlier observation remains interpretable. Do not repeatedly delete/reupload to chase an imagined distribution reset.

## Decision rules without arbitrary algorithm targets

- **Repeated comprehensibility problem:** if multiple episodes attract confusion about who said what or whether the story ended, revise story context, pacing or text readability before increasing volume. Comments are a limited sample, so verify the issue by replaying the actual video.
- **Opening weakness hypothesis:** if official continuation/watch-quality evidence and playback review both point to a slow opening across distinct episodes, test a more direct opening while retaining a similar content promise. Do not claim that the edit caused a change from one upload alone.
- **Large views, weaker watch-quality evidence:** retain the result as evidence of exposure, but do not select it as the business winner solely on that count. Check whether the source celebrity, topic or outside distribution differs.
- **One unusual success:** create a related but distinct episode to test repeatability. Do not immediately replace the channel's entire subject or publish near-duplicates.
- **Small/incomplete reports:** extend observation, review another comparable episode and keep the outcome inconclusive. Do not invent retention/geography data or lower a gate to force a result.
- **Rights/campaign problem:** pause that asset/source and surface the specific issue. Preserve the format as a research candidate; offer alternative sources without erasing the user's preference.
- **Free quota exhaustion or render backlog:** queue the next job, run an already approved local fallback or wait. Do not enable paid services, rotate accounts to evade quota or sacrifice factual/source checks to keep a streak.
- **Weak monetization route:** if access is absent, clips are repeatedly ineligible or no campaign/client has approved work, maintain zero revenue in the plan. Do not reclassify views as booked income.

These are bounded editorial/operational rules, not validated retention thresholds or formulas. No invented minimum swipe rate, target completion percentage, fixed best upload time or custom API-based quality score is required.

## Proposed 30/60/90-day execution

Day 1 means project execution starts after owner choices are recorded, not that the current research has already begun publishing. Gates take precedence over dates. If implementation/source eligibility is not ready, the corresponding checkpoint reports a delay instead of rushing output.

### Days 1–30: prove the pipeline and run the first cycle

**Days 1–7, if feasible:** choose the third entertainment slot; verify source access; establish channel/account boundaries; test the free writer/voice/render path; render one sample per proposed format; review technical and factual/rights results. Obtain an authorized private upload and retry/recovery check before enabling public scheduling. No complete production pipeline exists yet, so this is a target, not a seven-day implementation guarantee.

**Days 8–26, after gates pass:** publish the nine distinct episodes per channel at a modest schedule, for example three per channel per week over three weeks. Rotate formats through the chosen slots so a single format is not always assigned the same weekday. Treat this as a practical allocation experiment; different posts still do not receive identical audience exposure.

Use a clearly named US timezone such as America/New_York for the target slot and store UTC for execution; keep India-time visibility in the dashboard. Do not hard-code an IST offset through US daylight-saving transitions. Slot selection is an experiment and owner preference, not a claim about the algorithm or a way to force US distribution. Use relevant US-English examples, spelling, contexts and honest metadata; no false location or irrelevant location tags.

**Day 30 checkpoint:** report produced/published/held jobs, actual free-tool reliability, unresolved source/risk choices, available official audience results and zero/actual revenue status. Some last uploads will not yet have a full seven-day window. Finish their final observations around Day 33 or later; do not compare immature data with older uploads as equal.

Deliverables: 18 episode ledgers if all jobs cleared; an honest inventory of held/unproduced jobs otherwise; three reviews per published video where available; one next-cycle recommendation per channel. No claim of a statistically reliable winning niche or expected income date.

### Days 31–60: test repeatability and reduce production friction

Complete the missing seven-day observations first. Select at most two promising formats per channel for another small cycle, or keep all three inconclusive if evidence is weak. For each selected format, plan three new episodes with one documented production/editorial change; keep story/demo substance distinct. Maintain a small research allocation for the user's sourced formats whose permissions were unresolved.

Investigate one applicable revenue mechanism per channel with actual source/eligibility evidence: an eligible campaign brief for entertainment, or relevant brand/affiliate/fan-funding access for education. Check Indian payout eligibility through the legitimate account flow when authorized. A campaign listing is still not an accepted submission or a paid invoice. Do not promise a sponsorship by Day 60.

Deliverables: second-cycle comparisons using official values and matched windows, fewer avoidable retries, updated rights/source register and an explicit revenue-access status. If the process still consumes unacceptable owner effort, simplify the format or reduce cadence rather than hiding that effort.

### Days 61–90: decide what to operate, pause or expand

Choose whether each channel has a coherent repeatable series, a reliable free production path, manageable unresolved risks and an accessible business route. Expand a proven process only to the volume that free quotas and source supply support. If no promising pattern exists, revise the format promise or pause that series; do not declare success from publishing alone.

Consider an additional language or longer video only if the current production and quality path is stable and there is actual audience evidence. Recheck YouTube's current/future program terms and campaign/payment rules before planning next-quarter income. A 90-day calendar ending near February 2027 is affected by the announced YPP changes; the separate revenue report distinguishes current and future requirements.

Deliverables: per-channel continue/revise/pause recommendation, owned-metric evidence, owner-workload report, actual income ledger and a bounded next-quarter plan. The realistic outcome can still be no monetization or no cash received; do not fabricate success to justify the pilot.

## Per-channel income ledger

Keep the two channels and each revenue route separate. Record the source currency, contract/report period, fees and payout dates. A projected scenario belongs outside actual earnings.

| State | Evidence needed | What it does not mean |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scenario / forecast | Explicit view, eligibility, rate and timing assumptions | Not a booking, promise or earned balance |
| Booked / contracted | An actual accepted deal or contract with its conditions | A public campaign listing or brand inquiry is not booked work |
| Submitted / pending | Submission ID, post/deliverable, current review status | No guaranteed approval or payout |
| Approved / estimated | Campaign approval or official estimated channel earnings with relevant terms | Still subject to validation, adjustments, caps, returns or other stated conditions |
| Settled / available | Provider/AdSense payable or available-balance evidence | Not yet necessarily in the bank |
| Bank received | Actual payout receipt, amount/currency and date | Gross rates, wallet estimates and taxes are not automatically take-home profit |

Separately store official YPP eligible metrics, campaign counted views, gross earnings, creator deductions, payment/FX deductions and actual receipts. Avoid double counting the same contract across both channels, or adding a cumulative lifetime balance every month. A view can only enter each revenue mechanism according to that mechanism's actual terms. If a claim/chargeback reverses earnings, post an adjustment linked to the original item rather than overwriting history.

## Practical owner involvement and current limits

Research, original scripts, graphics, synthetic demonstrations, narration, editing, report collection and review proposals are intended automation responsibilities. Remaining account-owner actions include authentic sign-in/consent, identity/payment setup, contractual acceptance and the pending risk/format choices. Initial sample review and periodic exception handling may still be needed; no zero-oversight or guaranteed monetization claim is made.

No videos were rendered, tool runtimes benchmarked, analytics accounts connected or jobs scheduled by this document. Public references and official definitions inform the design; our own performance evidence begins only after an authorized pilot actually runs. All thresholds, campaign terms and report availability should be checked again at implementation and before the next measurement cycle.
