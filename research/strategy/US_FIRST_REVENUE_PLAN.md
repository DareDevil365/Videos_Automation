# US-first audience and revenue plan

Checked: 2026-11-01. This is a planning model for an India-operated business targeting US viewers. It records no personal account/payment information. There are no channel results, enrolled campaigns, affiliate approvals, sponsor offers, or sales. All positive revenue calculations below are conditional arithmetic, not expected income or a probability forecast. Figures use USD because the source rates do; INR receipts depend on actual settlement and foreign-exchange charges.

## Audience recommendation

Use US-first English for both channels initially. For education, make examples relevant to US students and early-career workers: a résumé rather than an assumed universal CV, a realistic job-email example, a useful AI task, or a source-backed study exercise. Treat employment requirements and software availability as location-specific. US relevance does not justify invented employment experience or a false creator identity.

For entertainment, begin with US-relevant creator/pop-culture interests and clear English. Compare US results with the UK, Canada and Australia rather than excluding those audiences automatically. Some researched campaigns explicitly accept these English-speaking markets. That gives a concrete commercial reason to explore them; it does not prove one nationality prefers brainrot.

We did not find sufficient evidence to rank countries by appetite for sigma edits, storytime or poster recommendations. View counts on example posts do not establish viewer geography or earnings. A larger audience at a lower per-view return may produce more total income, and a smaller task-focused audience may generate better sponsor/affiliate fit. These are business possibilities to test, not measured results for this project.

YouTube says advertiser competition varies by geography; it does not provide a fixed public US Shorts rate for this channel. It also says changing channel location does not determine where videos are recommended. Use relevant subject matter, language and audience response; no account-location trick or guaranteed US delivery is assumed. Posting windows are experiments, not a proven long-term growth switch. [Revenue geography](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314357?hl=en), [recommendation/location/timing FAQ](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/141805?hl=en).

## Published clipping rates and unit economics

An observed Iced Coffee Hour × Greg Lav podcast campaign quoted **$0.75 per 1,000 YouTube views**. Its source was readable earlier in the research but a later refresh failed; treat it as a dated lead, not a reserved open campaign. [Specific campaign](https://contentrewards.com/c/campaigns/a6e3a983-c8c7-4c4f-ac48-22022b391562).

The checked Split-Screen English campaign's **YouTube row quotes $1.50/1,000**, despite a $3 headline. It is a specific promotional UGC brief, not a rate payable to any gameplay or creator edit. Reference files and its complete rule remain join-gated. [Specific campaign](https://contentrewards.com/c/campaigns/6e8cb2c6-7724-44a4-83da-66697acb20af).

Content Rewards currently deducts 10% on CPM earnings. Its pricing describes seven earning days after approval plus a three-day hold; bank settlement follows separately. Approval wait, minimums, caps, rejected views, budget exhaustion, KYC and transfer/FX/tax are not removed by this arithmetic. [Pricing and timing](https://contentrewards.com/pricing/creators).

| Views actually counted under an approved brief | At $0.75 gross/1K, after 10% | At $1.50 gross/1K, after 10% |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 10,000 | $6.75 | $13.50 |
| 50,000 | $33.75 | $67.50 |
| 100,000 | $67.50 | $135.00 |

These are before transfer/FX/tax and assume all displayed views count under the brief, the cap is not reached, and money remains. Counted campaign views are not interchangeable with total channel views. Pool campaigns have no known fixed per-view return. Do not apply the same fee schedule to a different platform without checking it.

## Sponsor types and realistic pricing evidence

For education, investigate writing/AI/study/productivity software, learning providers and relevant career tools. Grammarly is a verified affiliate-application lead; that is not a verified fixed-fee sponsorship offer. Notion's affiliate page currently says new applications are closed. Other names in these categories are prospects until their programs and contact route are verified. [Affiliate research](../revenue/REVENUE_EDUCATION.md).

A directly inspected Kevin Stratvert Short displays a paid-promotion label and links to a Genspark tutorial. This establishes an observed software-brand commercial example, not its payment amount, our program access or a guarantee of organic reach. [Direct check and limits](../references/DIRECT_REFERENCE_CHECKS.md).

For entertainment, investigate game publishers, creator/podcast networks, media launches and compatible consumer apps. Existing campaign listings demonstrate examples of this demand, but a media brand listing is not a sponsor commitment or permission for unrelated clips. A career-software brand may prefer a relevant demonstration over an unrelated sigma edit even when the latter receives more views; this is an audience-fit hypothesis.

Aspire surveyed 1,200 creators and reports median quoted base rates. Its Shorts chart shows **$200 for the 2.5K–25K tier**, **$250 for 25K–60K**, and **$500 for 60K–250K**. These are survey prices, not deals promised to this project, not a rate for every faceless channel, and not a YouTube ad CPM. Usage rights, exclusivity and additional terms are separate. The chart was visually inspected during this research. [Aspire report and chart](https://www.aspire.io/blog/how-much-do-youtube-influencers-charge-for-sponsored-videos).

For another scoped reference, Collabstr reports a $255 average completed YouTube collaboration in its marketplace; it combines deliverables and is not a new-channel Shorts forecast. Its aggregate data also distinguishes asking rates from paid rates. Do not use its inconsistent year-over-year percentage as an input. [First-party marketplace report](https://collabstr.com/2026-influencer-marketing-report).

Therefore the financial model may test **one $200 sponsor Short**, but should book **$0 sponsor income until a real contract exists**. Companies generally negotiate for the audience, deliverable and rights; no specific company was found publicly promising a universal $200 fee to new channels. Include sales effort, revisions, reporting, disclosure, payment delay and possible nonpayment. Do not promise to contact or negotiate with sponsors automatically.

## Calendar: what to do, and what money would require

Windows start when implementation begins, not from the research date. They are proposed work windows, not committed development estimates or income deadlines. No current production pipeline exists. Each channel has its own eligibility and result history.

| Window | Operational checkpoint | Conditional monetary example | Budget assumption |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Weeks 1–2 | Prove one viable source/format per channel; private sample, render benchmark and supported account/payout path | No income modeled | $0 |
| Weeks 3–6 | Pilot distinct episodes, validate actual audience, optionally test one eligible campaign | 50K counted views at the observed $0.75 campaign rate would yield $33.75 after its assumed 10% fee | $0 until the campaign is approved and earnings settle |
| Months 2–3 | Keep the better-supported series, verify one affiliate or product route; prepare a media kit if audience evidence warrants it | 200K total counted campaign views at $0.75/1K would yield $135 after 10%, within applicable per-clip caps/budgets | Still permit $0; no sponsor cadence assumed |
| Months 4–6 | Reforecast using real reports; test a suitable sponsor offer if a brand agrees | A stronger monthly case of 400K counted campaign views at $0.75/1K gives $270 after 10%; one $200 sponsor adds $200 gross: $470 before remaining costs. Two such deals would make $670 | $0 remains plausible; the positive case is a target scenario, not an expected range |

The later scenarios are not a growth curve, a minimum, or mutually exclusive guaranteed stages. Views may come earlier, later or never; an audience may not qualify for the available briefs. The sponsor arithmetic mixes campaign earnings after their platform fee with a gross sponsor fee; neither figure is take-home profit or cash already in the bank. Do not also add total views as YouTube revenue without YPP eligibility and actual RPM.

Separately, ten sales of a hypothetical $5 template create $50 gross sales, not $50 profit. Platform/processor fees, refunds, tax and support remain. This is a pricing/quantity scenario, not a measured conversion forecast. Affiliate income stays zero in the budget until an actual agreement and conversion evidence exist; public views alone do not justify an assumed conversion percentage.

## Why YouTube ads are not our first-income assumption

Current ads/Premium entry requires 1K subscribers plus 10M qualified Shorts views in 90 days or 4K qualified long-form hours in 12 months, followed by review. Earlier fan-funding access is a separate threshold. [Current YPP requirements](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851?hl=en).

The official announcement changes new entry from **February 1, 2027** to 1K subscribers plus **20M Shorts views/90 days or 8K long-form hours/365 days**. It also announces a 10M/90-day ongoing monthly Shorts Creator Pool condition. A six-month plan from late 2026 must include that change. [Official announced changes](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12843009).

At three Shorts per channel per week, roughly 39 new posts fit a 90-day window. If all qualifying views came from those posts, reaching 10M would average about 256K each, and 20M about 513K each. This is threshold arithmetic, not the performance of our videos; older posts can also receive views during the window. The two channels' numbers cannot be pooled into one channel's entry target.

No reliable channel-specific Shorts RPM was established. Thus ad revenue remains **$0 in the launch budget**, and the future model uses actual eligible views and reported owned-channel revenue once available.

## Reforecast and decision points

At the first 4-, 8- and 12-week reviews, record actual publication count, format/series, content/source status, available official views and viewing metrics, actual audience geography where reported, campaign approved/rejected/settled amounts, actual affiliate clicks/conversions if provided by the program, product refunds/fees and real owner/support time. Keep unavailable data unknown.

The US-first preference is recorded. Specific acceptance of copyright/YPP/repetition/identity/accuracy/automation/business risks is not. Preserve the entire format catalog while decisions remain open. No false foreign account, paid boost, project/key rotation, fake engagement or automatic purchase is required by the plan.

The next defensible positive forecast requires real production feasibility, permitted asset access, payout approval and an initial audience sample. A zero-service-spend prototype can still use electricity, existing equipment and the user's time for identity/contract decisions. A system that creates videos without filming is feasible to investigate; a profitable business needing no accountable operator has not been demonstrated.
