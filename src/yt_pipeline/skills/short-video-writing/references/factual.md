# Evidence-led explainers

Start with one answerable question or supported contrast. Use a causal explanation and a concrete example rather than a list of impressive claims. Separate what sources show from an analogy, inference or proposal. A clearly hypothetical demonstration can explain a concept; it cannot invent a real result, price, benchmark, quote or historical event.

Tie the opening promise to the answer actually delivered. Explain a necessary term before relying on it. Choose visuals that can demonstrate the explanation with sources, measured diagrams or honest illustration. Do not import fictional mystery pacing at the expense of clarity, or use an unrelated trending fact as the title/description.

End once the stated question is answered. An actionable example is useful when the video promises one. Unsupported certainty, outcome guarantees and a framework that never demonstrates the promised answer fail review. Preserve the application's evidence and citation contracts.
