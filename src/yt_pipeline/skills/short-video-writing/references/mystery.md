# Mystery and thriller: fair uncertainty

Make one concrete fact contradict the expected situation. Before writing the threat, decide who sent it, what they want, why now, and why those exact words serve their goal. Knowing the sender's identity is not the same as explaining the opening behavior.

Plant an ordinary detail early that can be interpreted two ways. Let each new message update the viewer's hypothesis. Characters should investigate or make a meaningful choice rather than exchange generic warnings. Restrict private knowledge to the actual conversations. Do not use ordinary SMS as unexplained control over doors, breakers or remote machinery.

Move between conversations when new information changes what the protagonist should ask next. A timestamp should establish chronology or meaningful delay; a battery warning or blackout should have a story function. Resolve the principal opening question before an optional further danger/cliffhanger. Do not substitute an arbitrary "Help me" notification for the explanation.

Vary pace: quick urgent questions, a short wait when an answer is genuinely uncertain, then a clear reveal. Silence is strongest when the viewer has a question to carry through it. Preserve a phone blackout as actual silence; do not run the music through an intended silence beat. Avoid a sting on every message or suspense effects that reveal the twist before its words.

Failure check: reconstruct the cause and character knowledge without adding facts. Name the exact early clue that supports the explanation. If a reader must invent a motive, extra device or unseen coincidence, revise the story.
